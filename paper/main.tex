\begin{abstract}
Most production safety guards are billion-parameter LLMs fine-tuned to emit binary safe/unsafe verdicts---accurate but GPU-bound and opaque at decision level. We ask: can a small classifier atop a frozen encoder match this accuracy if the representation space is geometrically structured for the task? We reformulate safety classification geometrically: a 7.3M-parameter projection head reorganizes frozen Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B (1024-d) vectors into a 256-d intent-separable subspace, driven by a structured objective combining prototype-anchored Sinkhorn optimal transport, hierarchical contrastive supervision, and feature decorrelation; hyperbolic layers on the Poincar\'{e} ball serve as a complementary inductive bias activated specifically by this objective. A 0.1M-parameter residual classifier is then trained on the frozen subspace. The full stack totals $\sim$607M parameters---the smallest among all 9 production-grade safety guards we benchmark (1B--9B)---while outperforming them on 8 of 9 datasets. With pre-computed embeddings, trainable heads exceed 50 samples/s on CPU. Our model is trained exclusively on a proprietary corpus fully disjoint---at instance, source, and annotation level---from all public benchmarks. It wins 80 of 81 (98.8\%) pairwise accuracy comparisons against 9 production-grade guards spanning WildGuard, Llama-Guard-3, ShieldGemma, Qwen3Guard, Granite-Guardian, and Beaver-Dam families, with a mean lead of +12.3 points overall and +14.0 on intent-sensitive subsets. Experiments confirm that hyperbolic geometry alone is inert under standard BCE loss; its representational power is only activated by the full structured objective. We further connect hyperbolic projection to dimension-efficient safety taxonomy embedding (Proposition~\ref{prop:hyp-dim}) and the Sinkhorn objective to a margin-based generalization bound (Proposition~\ref{prop:ot-margin}). These results establish that geometry-aware embedding classification can match or exceed billion-scale generative guards at a fraction of the inference cost.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
%========================================================================

Content safety is critical as LLMs are deployed at scale. Post-hoc safety moderation encompasses two paradigms. \textbf{Encoder-based discriminative classifiers}---OpenAI Moderation API, Perspective API, Detoxify, HateBERT---use lightweight transformer encoders for low-latency scoring but offer limited contextual reasoning. \textbf{Generative guard models}---WildGuard~\cite{han2024wildguard}, Llama~Guard~3~\cite{inan2023llama}, ShieldGemma, Qwen3Guard-Gen, Granite~Guardian---fine-tune 1--9B parameter LLMs to output binary safety verdicts, achieving strong accuracy at the cost of billion-parameter inference per query. Both paradigms share a blind spot: they discriminate safe from unsafe content but reveal neither \emph{why} a decision was made nor the geometric structure underlying it.

A third, emerging line studies safety through \textbf{representation geometry}. Zou et al.~\cite{zou2023representation} showed that harmfulness concepts are linearly separable in LLM hidden states. Arditi et al.~\cite{arditi2024refusal} identified a single refusal-mediating direction. Lee et al.~\cite{lee2024mechanistic} probed harmful intent in embedding spaces. These findings suggest safety-relevant structure exists in representation space. However, prior representation-based safety classifiers have not closed the accuracy gap with large generative guards, and their geometric design choices---Euclidean linear probing, single-direction interventions---have not been benchmarked against the hierarchical nature of safety taxonomies.

We argue that the core obstacle is \emph{representation collapse}: frozen embedding spaces such as Qwen3-Embedding~\cite{qwen3} lack the structural priors to separate malicious intent from benign discussion of the same topic. As Figure~\ref{fig:tsne} (left) shows, the original 1,024-d space exhibits near-zero silhouette score ($\approx 0.008$), with safe, pseudo-harmful, and malicious content completely overlapping. The consequence is \textbf{intent blindness}---classifiers conflate ``How do I build a bomb to hurt people?'' with ``What are the dangers of building bombs?''---producing over-censorship measurable on benchmarks such as XSTest.

To address this, we propose a two-stage framework: a geometrically principled projection head learns an intent-aware representation space, then a neural classifier is trained on the frozen projected representations. Four design choices advance beyond prior representation-based classifiers: (1)~a \textbf{structured loss} combining hierarchical contrastive supervision, prototype-anchored Sinkhorn optimal transport, and Barlow-Twins feature decorrelation---the dominant source of gain in our ablations; (2)~a \textbf{hyperbolic projection} on the Poincar\'{e} ball~\cite{nickel2017poincare,ganea2018hyperbolic} effective specifically in conjunction with the structured loss; (3)~a \textbf{learnable Euclidean--hyperbolic gate} $\beta$ with skip connection; and (4)~a \textbf{residual neural classifier} (0.1M parameters) with mixup~\cite{zhang2018mixup} and temperature scaling~\cite{guo2017calibration}. The total trainable footprint is 7.4M parameters; with pre-computed embeddings the pipeline runs at $>$50 samples/s on CPU.

On data provenance: our 389K training corpus is sourced from an internal industrial moderation pipeline and shares no upstream provenance with any of the 9 public benchmarks we evaluate on. Reported gains thus reflect representation-level transfer to unseen distributions rather than in-distribution memorization.

Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
    \item We formalize \emph{intent blindness} as a measurable consequence of representation collapse and show hyperbolic geometry directly addresses it.
    \item We demonstrate that a residual neural classifier on a hyperbolic-structured frozen subspace closes the accuracy gap to billion-parameter generative guard models.
    \item We establish that the structured loss is necessary: hyperbolic geometry alone is inert under standard BCE and only becomes useful through interaction with the structured loss.
    \item \textbf{Smallest end-to-end footprint among all baselines.} DeepSafe's inference pipeline totals $\sim$607M parameters (0.6B frozen encoder + 7.4M trainable heads), strictly smaller than every baseline---Llama-Guard-3-1B (1.0B), ShieldGemma-2B (2.0B), and the 4B/7B/8B/9B variants across WildGuard, Qwen3Guard, ShieldGemma, Granite-Guardian, and Beaver-Dam families. After embedding pre-computation, trainable heads exceed 50 samples/s on CPU, requiring no GPU at inference.
\item \textbf{Theoretical grounding.} We prove two propositions justifying our central design choices: a tree-embedding dimension bound distinguishing Euclidean from Poincar\'{e} projection (Proposition~\ref{prop:hyp-dim}), and an OT--margin--generalization chain showing the Sinkhorn objective directly controls a margin-based generalization bound (Proposition~\ref{prop:ot-margin}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
%========================================================================

\subsection{Post-Hoc Safety Classification}

Post-hoc safety moderation encompasses two paradigms: \textbf{encoder-based discriminative classifiers} (OpenAI Moderation API, Perspective API, Detoxify, HateBERT) offering low latency but limited reasoning, and \textbf{generative guard models} (WildGuard~\cite{han2024wildguard}, Llama~Guard~3~\cite{inan2023llama}, ShieldGemma, Qwen3Guard-Gen, Granite~Guardian, Beaver-Dam) that fine-tune 1--9B LLMs for binary verdicts at billion-parameter inference cost~\cite{markov2023holistic}. Pre-deployment alignment (RLHF~\cite{ouyang2022training}, DPO~\cite{rafailov2024direct}, constitutional AI~\cite{bai2022constitutional}) aims for safer generation but provides no explicit post-hoc judgments. Our method operates on a geometrically reorganized representation space, combining encoder-level latency with generative-guard accuracy.

\subsection{Representation Engineering for Safety}

Recent work studies safety through representation geometry. Zou et al.~\cite{zou2023representation} demonstrated linear separability of harmfulness in LLM hidden states. Arditi et al.~\cite{arditi2024refusal} identified a single refusal-mediating direction. These findings establish that safety structure exists in representation space. However, existing approaches use raw embedding spaces with linear probes, inheriting the representation collapse shown in Figure~\ref{fig:tsne}, and have not been benchmarked against hierarchical safety taxonomies. Our work reorganizes the space through hyperbolic projection and trains a nonlinear classifier on the structured subspace.

\subsection{Contrastive Learning and Counterfactuals}

Supervised contrastive learning~\cite{khosla2020supervised} and its NLP adaptations~\cite{gao2021simcse} learn structured representations from labeled data. Counterfactual augmentation improves robustness~\cite{kaushik2020learning}. Our hierarchical contrastive loss operates on multiple label granularities, and we incorporate 4,993 counterfactual pairs into training via a repulsion loss targeting intent-level separation (\S\ref{sec:cf_loss}).

\section{Method}
%========================================================================

\subsection{Problem Formulation}

Let $\mathcal{D} = \{(\mathbf{x}_i, y_i^b, y_i^I)\}_{i=1}^N$ be a dataset where $y_i^b \in \{0, 1\}$ is a binary safety label and $y_i^I \in \{0, 1, 2\}$ follows our hierarchical taxonomy: Safe (0), Pseudo-Harmful (1: sensitive topics with legitimate purpose), and Malicious (2: explicitly harmful). Safe and Pseudo-Harmful map to $y^b = 0$; Malicious maps to $y^b = 1$. Let $f_\theta(\mathbf{x}) = \mathbf{e} \in \mathbb{R}^{1024}$ be a frozen Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B~\cite{qwen3} encoder. We learn a projection head $g_\phi: \mathbb{R}^{1024} \to \mathbb{R}^{256}$ and a neural classifier $h_\psi: \mathbb{R}^{256} \to [0,1]$.

\subsection{Projection Head Architecture}
\label{sec:proj}

The projection head uses dual hyperbolic layers with a learnable Euclidean-hyperbolic gate:

\paragraph{Dual Hyperbolic Neural Projection.} Safety labels form a natural hierarchy (Safe $\to$ Pseudo-Harmful $\to$ Malicious). Euclidean geometry poorly captures such structure, as volume grows polynomially with radius while hierarchical descendants grow exponentially~\cite{nickel2017poincare}. We project embeddings into the Poincar\'{e} ball ($c=1.0$), where volume grows exponentially and naturally encodes tree-like hierarchies.

Given a frozen embedding $\mathbf{e} \in \mathbb{R}^{1024}$, we first L2-normalize to $\tilde{\mathbf{e}} = \mathbf{e} / \|\mathbf{e}\|_2$, then project to a tangent space $\mathbf{t} = \text{LayerNorm}(\mathbf{W}_{\text{in}} \tilde{\mathbf{e}})$. The exponential map $\exp_{\mathbf{0}}^c(\mathbf{t})$ lifts $\mathbf{t}$ onto the Poincar\'{e} ball:

\begin{align}
    \exp_{\mathbf{0}}^c(\mathbf{t}) = \tanh(\sqrt{c} \, \|\mathbf{t}\|) \frac{\mathbf{t}}{\sqrt{c} \, \|\mathbf{t}\|}
\end{align}

Within the hyperbolic space, we apply \textbf{two stacked} hyperbolic linear layers with GELU activations, following the hyperbolic neural network framework of~\cite{ganea2018hyperbolic}. Each hyperbolic linear layer applies M\"{o}bius matrix-vector multiplication $\mathbf{W} \otimes_c \mathbf{x} = \exp_{\mathbf{0}}^c(\mathbf{W} \log_{\mathbf{0}}^c(\mathbf{x}))$, followed by hyperbolic activation. After the hyperbolic layers, we apply the logarithmic map to return to Euclidean space:

\begin{align}
    \log_{\mathbf{0}}^c(\mathbf{x}) = \frac{\operatorname{arctanh}(\sqrt{c} \, \|\mathbf{x}\|)}{\sqrt{c} \, \|\mathbf{x}\|} \mathbf{x}
\end{align}

The hyperbolic-to-Euclidean transition layer concatenates the log-mapped features with their distance-to-origin in the Poincar\'{e} ball, which encodes a learned ``severity'' metric.

\paragraph{Learnable Gate Mechanism.} The hyperbolic pathway captures hierarchical safety structure, while a parallel Euclidean residual pathway preserves fine-grained semantic information. A learnable scalar gate $\beta = \sigma(w_\beta)$ adaptively blends the two:

\begin{align}
    \mathbf{h}_{\text{combined}} = \beta \cdot \mathbf{h}_{\text{hyp}} + (1-\beta) \cdot \mathbf{h}_{\text{res}} + \mathbf{W}_{\text{skip}} \tilde{\mathbf{e}}
\end{align}

The skip connection $\mathbf{W}_{\text{skip}} \tilde{\mathbf{e}}$ preserves input-level information and prevents vanishing signals through the deep projection.

The combined features are processed through LayerNorm, GELU activation, dropout (0.1), and a final linear projection to 256 dimensions, with batch normalization and L2 normalization producing the final output $\mathbf{z} \in \mathbb{S}^{255}$.

\subsection{Neural Classifier}

Unlike prior work that trains a logistic regression probe, DeepSafe trains a \textbf{residual neural classifier} $h_\psi$ on the frozen projected representations:

\begin{align}
    \mathbf{h}_1 &= \text{GELU}(\text{LayerNorm}(\mathbf{W}_1 \mathbf{z} + \mathbf{b}_1)) \quad (256 \to 256) \\
    \mathbf{h}_2 &= \mathbf{h}_1 + \text{GELU}(\text{LayerNorm}(\mathbf{W}_2 \mathbf{h}_1 + \mathbf{b}_2)) \quad (256 \to 128, \text{ residual}) \\
    \mathbf{h}_3 &= \text{GELU}(\text{LayerNorm}(\mathbf{W}_3 \mathbf{h}_2 + \mathbf{b}_3)) \quad (128 \to 64) \\
    \text{logits} &= \mathbf{W}_4 \mathbf{h}_3 + \mathbf{b}_4 \quad (64 \to 2)
\end{align}

Training uses AdamW with cosine annealing, class-weighted loss to handle label imbalance, mixup augmentation~\cite{zhang2018mixup} ($\alpha=0.2$) on projected embeddings, and temperature scaling~\cite{guo2017calibration} ($T=1.5$) for calibrated probabilities. The classifier adds only $\sim$0.1M parameters and is trained for up to 50 epochs with early stopping (patience 10).

\paragraph{Spectral Decorrelation.} A Barlow Twins-style~\cite{zbontar2021barlow} regularization penalizes off-diagonal elements of the feature correlation matrix, promoting non-redundant feature dimensions:

\begin{align}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{decorr}} = \lambda_d \sum_{i \neq j} \left(\frac{1}{B} \mathbf{Z}^\top \mathbf{Z}\right)_{ij}^2
\end{align}

\subsection{Optimal Transport Class Regularization}

Standard contrastive loss operates at the instance level and can be sensitive to outliers. We introduce a distribution-level objective using entropic optimal transport~\cite{cuturi2013sinkhorn}. The debiased Sinkhorn divergence~\cite{feydy2019interpolating}, defined as $S_\epsilon(P, Q) = \text{OT}_\epsilon(P, Q) - \tfrac{1}{2}\text{OT}_\epsilon(P, P) - \tfrac{1}{2}\text{OT}_\epsilon(Q, Q)$, is positive-definite and satisfies $S_\epsilon(P, P) = 0$. The intra-class term is interpreted as the empirical-distribution self-divergence converging to 0 as classes contract:

\begin{align}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{OT}} = \sum_{c} S_\epsilon(\mathbf{Z}_c, \mathbf{Z}_c) - \lambda_{\text{inter}} \sum_{a < b} S_\epsilon(\mathbf{Z}_a, \mathbf{Z}_b)
\end{align}

where $S_\epsilon$ is the Sinkhorn divergence computed via the Sinkhorn-Knopp algorithm with entropic regularization $\epsilon$. The first term promotes intra-class compactness, while the second term encourages inter-class separation at the distribution level.

\subsection{Hierarchical Contrastive Loss}

We retain the hierarchical contrastive loss~\cite{khosla2020supervised} operating at two granularities:

\begin{align}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{hier}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{bin}} + \alpha \cdot \mathcal{L}_{\text{intent}}
\end{align}

where $\mathcal{L}_{\text{bin}}$ contrasts safe vs.\ unsafe samples, and $\mathcal{L}_{\text{intent}}$ contrasts the three fine-grained intent classes (Safe, Pseudo-Harmful, Malicious).

\subsection{Learnable Prototype Anchors}

To provide explicit structural guidance, we maintain learnable prototype vectors $\{\mathbf{p}_k\}_{k=0}^{2}$ in the projected space~\cite{snell2017proto}, one per intent class, with hierarchical margins reflecting the intent taxonomy:

\begin{align}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{proto}} = -\underbrace{\frac{1}{B}\sum_i \cos(\mathbf{z}_i, \mathbf{p}_{y_i})}_{\text{attraction}} + \lambda_p \underbrace{\frac{1}{B}\sum_i \sum_{c \neq y_i} \max(0, \cos(\mathbf{z}_i, \mathbf{p}_c) - m_{y_i,c})}_{\text{hierarchical repulsion}}
\end{align}

where the margin matrix $m_{y,c}$ encodes the semantic distance between intent classes: Safe$\leftrightarrow$Malicious uses the largest margin (0.5), Safe$\leftrightarrow$Pseudo-Harmful uses a smaller margin (0.25), and Pseudo-Harmful$\leftrightarrow$Malicious uses an intermediate margin (0.35).

\subsection{Counterfactual Pair Repulsion}
\label{sec:cf_loss}

To sharpen intent-level separation, we augment training with 4,993 LLM-generated counterfactual pairs $(\mathbf{x}_i, \mathbf{x}_i^{\text{cf}})$, where one sample is harmful and its counterfactual is benign (or vice versa). Each pair is embedded and projected to $(\mathbf{z}_i, \mathbf{z}_i^{\text{cf}})$. The counterfactual loss applies hinge repulsion on cosine similarity:

\begin{align}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{cf}} = \frac{1}{N_{\text{cf}}} \sum_{i=1}^{N_{\text{cf}}} \max\!\big(0,\; \cos(\mathbf{z}_i, \mathbf{z}_i^{\text{cf}}) - m_{\text{cf}}\big)
\end{align}

where $m_{\text{cf}} = -0.3$ is the repulsion margin, ensuring counterfactual pairs are pushed apart in the projected space. The weight $\gamma = 0.5$ balances this term against the hierarchical and OT objectives. This loss directly targets the ``intent blindness'' pathology described in \S\ref{sec:intro}: a query like ``How do I build a bomb to hurt people?'' is pushed away from ``What are the dangers of building bombs?'' in the projected representation, making them separable by the downstream classifier.

\subsection{Full Training Objective}

\begin{align}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{DeepSafe}} = \underbrace{\mathcal{L}_{\text{bin}} + \alpha \, \mathcal{L}_{\text{intent}}}_{\mathcal{L}_{\text{hier}}} + \lambda_{\text{ot}} \Big(\underbrace{\sum_{c} S_\epsilon(\mathbf{Z}_c, \mathbf{Z}_c) - \lambda_{\text{inter}} \sum_{a < b} S_\epsilon(\mathbf{Z}_a, \mathbf{Z}_b)}_{\mathcal{L}_{\text{OT}}}\Big) + \lambda_p \mathcal{L}_{\text{proto}} + \lambda_d \mathcal{L}_{\text{decorr}} + \gamma \, \mathcal{L}_{\text{cf}}
\end{align}

We use $\lambda_{\text{ot}}=0.3$, $\lambda_{\text{inter}}=0.5$, $\lambda_p=0.2$, $\alpha=2.0$, and $\lambda_d=0.005$. Training uses AdamW with cosine annealing (1e-3 to 1e-6) for up to 100 epochs with early stopping (patience 15).

\subsection{Theoretical Properties}
\label{sec:theory}

We provide two formal properties that ground the two main design choices of DeepSafe---the hyperbolic projection and the Sinkhorn OT regularizer---on established results in metric embedding and statistical learning theory. Full proofs are deferred to Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.

\paragraph{Setup.} Let $\mathcal{T}$ denote the safety taxonomy, modeled as a rooted tree where the root branches into the three top-level intent classes $\{\text{Safe}, \text{Pseudo-Harmful}, \text{Malicious}\}$, and each top-level class may further branch into $k$ fine-grained sub-classes (e.g., harm categories, jailbreak families). Let $n = |\mathcal{T}|$ denote the total number of nodes. Tree distances $d_\mathcal{T}(u,v)$ are weighted shortest-path distances. We say a map $\phi: \mathcal{T} \to (\mathcal{M}, d_\mathcal{M})$ has multiplicative distortion $D \ge 1$ if there exists $C>0$ such that for all $u,v$:
\[
C \cdot d_\mathcal{T}(u,v) \le d_\mathcal{M}(\phi(u),\phi(v)) \le C D \cdot d_\mathcal{T}(u,v).
\]

\paragraph{Scope of Proposition~1.} While our current taxonomy uses three intent classes, real-world safety taxonomies expand to dozens of fine-grained sub-categories (e.g., the AegisAI taxonomy contains 12 harm categories, WildGuardMix enumerates 13 risk types). Proposition~\ref{prop:hyp-dim} characterizes the asymptotic dimension cost of preserving such hierarchies, motivating hyperbolic projection as a future-proof choice that absorbs taxonomy growth without re-dimensioning.

\begin{proposition}[Hyperbolic dimension advantage for safety taxonomies]
\label{prop:hyp-dim}
Let $\mathcal{T}$ be a safety taxonomy tree as above with $n$ leaves and minimum branching factor $b \ge 2$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] Any embedding $\phi: \mathcal{T} \to (\mathbb{R}^d, \|\cdot\|_2)$ with multiplicative distortion at most $D$ requires
\[
d \;=\; \Omega\!\left(\frac{\log n}{\log D}\right).
\]
\item[(b)] For the Poincar\'{e} ball model $(\mathbb{B}^d_c, d_{\mathbb{B}})$ with curvature $-c<0$, for any $\varepsilon>0$ there exists an embedding with multiplicative distortion $1+\varepsilon$ and dimension
\[
d \;=\; O\!\left(\varepsilon^{-2}\right),
\]
independent of $n$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\textbf{Interpretation.} As safety practice expands the taxonomy with new sub-categories (e.g., adding emerging jailbreak families or fine-grained harm tags), Euclidean projection heads must grow their dimensionality to preserve the hierarchical metric, whereas a Poincar\'{e}-ball projection of fixed dimension can absorb arbitrarily large taxonomies at the same distortion. This is the formal counterpart of the heuristic ``exponential volume mirrors hierarchical descendants'' argument, and it is the property the dual-hyperbolic projection in Section~\ref{sec:proj} is designed to exploit.

\paragraph{From OT regularization to generalization.} The Sinkhorn objective $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{OT}}$ in Eq.~(9) is often introduced on heuristic grounds. We show it implies a learnable lower bound on classification margin, and hence on generalization error.

\begin{proposition}[OT--margin--generalization chain]
\label{prop:ot-margin}
Let $\{Z_c\}_{c \in \{0,1,2\}}$ denote the empirical feature distributions of the three intent classes on the unit hypersphere $\mathbb{S}^{255}$, and assume each has Euclidean diameter at most $2r_c$. Let $S_\varepsilon$ denote the entropy-regularized Sinkhorn divergence with regularization $\varepsilon>0$, and suppose that for all $a \ne b$,
\[
S_\varepsilon(Z_a, Z_b) \;\ge\; \Delta.
\]
Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] (\textbf{Margin}.) There exists a 1-Lipschitz function $f: \mathbb{S}^{255} \to \mathbb{R}$ separating classes $a$ and $b$ with population margin
\[
\gamma_{a,b} \;\ge\; \tfrac{1}{2}\Delta \;-\; (r_a + r_b) \;-\; C_1\sqrt{\varepsilon},
\]
for a universal constant $C_1>0$.
\item[(ii)] (\textbf{Generalization}.) For a sample of size $n$ drawn i.i.d.\ from $\tfrac12(P_a + P_b)$ where $P_c$ is the population law of class $c$, the binary classification error of $f$ satisfies, with probability $\ge 1-\delta$,
\[
\mathrm{err}(f) \;\le\; \frac{C_2}{\gamma_{a,b}\sqrt{n}} \;+\; \sqrt{\frac{\log(1/\delta)}{2n}},
\]
for a universal constant $C_2>0$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\textbf{Interpretation.} Minimizing the inter-class term $-\sum_{a<b} S_\varepsilon(Z_a, Z_b)$ in Eq.~(9) directly increases $\Delta$, which in turn raises the margin lower bound $\gamma_{a,b}$ in (i) and tightens the generalization bound in (ii). Likewise, the intra-class term $\sum_c S_\varepsilon(Z_c, Z_c)$ shrinks $r_c$. This explains why the DeepSafe objective provides gains beyond instance-level contrastive losses: it directly optimizes a quantity that controls a margin-based generalization bound at the \emph{distribution} level. Combined with Proposition~\ref{prop:hyp-dim}, this gives a principled---rather than purely empirical---reason to expect the structured loss to interact synergistically with the hyperbolic projection: the geometry provides the dimension-efficient substrate, the OT loss extracts the margin.

\subsection{Why DeepSafe Works: Component Analysis}

Before discussing component-level gains, we make explicit a finding that constrains the interpretation of \Cref{tab:ablation}: when trained under standard BCE loss with capacity matched, replacing Euclidean projection with hyperbolic projection yields no consistent accuracy gain (80.0\% vs.\ 81.1\%; AUC 0.935 vs.\ 0.933, Figure~\ref{fig:ablation}). This indicates that hyperbolic geometry contributes by expanding the hypothesis class in a way the structured DeepSafe objective can exploit, not by acting as a stand-alone geometric prior. The factorial interaction---hyperbolic projection under DeepSafe loss versus Euclidean projection under DeepSafe loss---establishes the geometry $\times$ loss synergy as the principal source of gain. \Cref{tab:ablation}'s gains attributed to ``hyperbolic projection'' should therefore be understood as joint gains from added capacity plus the structured loss being able to use that capacity.

\Cref{tab:ablation} quantifies the contribution of each architectural innovation. Figure~\ref{fig:ablation} provides a complementary view, showing that under standard BCE loss, Euclidean and hyperbolic projections perform comparably; the gains emerge only from the synergy between hyperbolic geometry and the structured DeepSafe loss.

\subsection{Representation Quality and Inference Efficiency}

DeepSafe's projection head fundamentally reorganizes the representation space: silhouette score rises from $\approx 0.008$ (raw embedding) to $\approx 0.30$ (DeepSafe-projected) for binary labels, and from $\approx 0.008$ to $\approx 0.27$ for 3-class intent labels---confirming that the geometric reorganization is not a trivial artefact of the binary objective. The hyperbolic pathway's distance-to-origin encodes safety severity, with malicious content mapped closer to the Poincar\'{e} ball boundary and safe content near the origin.

DeepSafe's full stack totals $\sim$607M parameters---the smallest among all 9 baselines (Table~\ref{tab:param_footprint}): $\sim$40\% smaller than Llama-Guard-3-1B (1.0B) and $\sim$15$\times$ smaller than ShieldGemma-9B (9.0B). With pre-computed embeddings, the projection + classifier exceed 50 samples/s on CPU (Figure~\ref{fig:speed_vs_accuracy}). The framework is favorable in both (a)~latency-sensitive online moderation and (b)~batch / pre-indexed pipelines where embedding cost is amortized across downstream tasks.

%========================================================================
\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
%========================================================================

The strong performance of DeepSafe derives from the synergistic combination of hyperbolic geometry, distribution-level OT supervision, learnable prototypes, and the residual neural classifier. Crucially, no single component accounts for the full gain: the hyperbolic projection establishes a representation space that the structured loss exploits, while under standard BCE loss hyperbolic and Euclidean projections perform comparably (Figure~\ref{fig:ablation}). Our findings thus support a narrower claim than ``hyperbolic geometry solves safety classification'': (i)~the structured loss (OT + prototypes + hierarchical contrastive + decorrelation + counterfactual repulsion) is the dominant lever; (ii)~the hyperbolic pathway is inert under BCE and only becomes useful in combination with the structured loss; (iii)~the 3-class taxonomy functions as a training regularizer, not a runtime decision surface. We stress these distinctions for follow-up work that may otherwise over-attribute gains to hyperbolic geometry alone.

\subsection{Limitations}

\paragraph{Encoder and embedding dependency.} DeepSafe requires one forward pass through the 0.6B Qwen3-Embedding encoder. While the 607M total footprint is already the smallest in our comparison set (Table~\ref{tab:param_footprint}), further reduction via distillation or quantization is left to future work. Performance is bounded by the encoder's quality; fine-tuning the encoder jointly or using a safety-specific encoder may yield further gains. The framework is strictly favorable when the encoder is already deployed for other tasks (semantic search, clustering, RAG), since safety classification then reuses an existing forward pass at near-zero marginal cost.

\paragraph{Geometry is not the lever.} As shown in Figure~\ref{fig:ablation}, hyperbolic projection alone (BCE loss, capacity-matched) does not outperform Euclidean projection. The contribution of geometry is conditional on the structured loss; readers should not interpret our results as evidence that hyperbolic geometry is a generally superior choice for binary safety classification.

\paragraph{Other limitations.} The Poincar\'{e} ball curvature $c=1.0$ is fixed; learning curvature adaptively or using mixed-curvature products~\cite{gu2018mixed} could improve representation quality. Our 4,993 counterfactual pairs are English-only and generated by a single LLM---scaling to multilingual domains could further improve intent-level separation. Evaluation covers English and (partially) Chinese safety domains; broader multilingual and multimodal safety evaluation remains future work.

\subsection{Broader Impacts}
\label{sec:broader}

Positive impact: lightweight, privacy-preserving safety filtering deployable on CPU, making content moderation accessible to smaller organizations and resource-constrained settings. Potential risks: like any safety classifier, the model may exhibit false positives/negatives, and adversaries could attempt circumvention. We recommend deployment as part of a layered defense, not as a standalone guarantee. The model is released under a restricted-use license with a calibrated threshold tuning guide to help deployers balance error types for their use case.

%========================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
%========================================================================

We introduced DeepSafe, a two-stage safety classification framework combining a geometrically principled hyperbolic projection head with a neural classifier. Our key innovations are: (1) dual hyperbolic layers encoding hierarchical safety taxonomy, (2) a learnable Euclidean--hyperbolic gate with skip connections, (3) optimal transport regularization for distribution-level class separation, (4) learnable prototype anchors with hierarchical margins, and (5) a residual neural classifier with mixup augmentation.

Across 9 held-out benchmarks, DeepSafe achieves the best accuracy on 8 of 9, winning 80 of 81 (98.8\%) pairwise comparisons against 9 production-grade generative guards---all while having the smallest inference footprint ($\sim$607M parameters) of any model compared. The central finding is that the structured loss is the dominant lever; hyperbolic geometry alone is inert under standard BCE and only becomes useful through interaction with the structured objective. We further provide formal grounding: hyperbolic projection enables dimension-efficient safety taxonomy embedding (Proposition~\ref{prop:hyp-dim}), and the Sinkhorn objective directly controls a margin-based generalization bound (Proposition~\ref{prop:ot-margin}). DeepSafe establishes that geometrically structured frozen embeddings, combined with a learned neural classifier, can match or exceed billion-scale generative guards at a fraction of the inference cost, opening the door to fast, private, and cost-effective safety filtering for production LLM deployments.

\subsection{Proofs}

\subsubsection{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:hyp-dim}}

\paragraph{Part (a): Euclidean lower bound.}
The result is a direct consequence of Bourgain's embedding theorem and its tightness for tree metrics~\cite{linial1995geometry,bourgain1985lipschitz}. For a complete $b$-ary tree of $n$ leaves, any embedding into $\ell_2^d$ has multiplicative distortion
\[
D \;\ge\; \Omega\!\left(\sqrt{\log n}\right)
\]
when $d$ is unconstrained, and a standard volume / Johnson--Lindenstrauss-style argument gives that achieving distortion $D$ requires
\[
d \;\ge\; \Omega\!\left(\frac{\log n}{\log D}\right).
\]
Specifically, for any pair of leaves $u,v$ at tree-distance $h$, the embedded points must lie on a ball of radius $CDh$ around their tree-LCA, while the $b^h$ leaves of any subtree must remain pairwise at Euclidean distance at least $C h$. A standard packing bound in $\mathbb{R}^d$ then forces $d = \Omega(h \log b / \log D)$, and taking $h = \Theta(\log_b n)$ yields the claim. \qed

\paragraph{Part (b): Hyperbolic upper bound.}
This is Theorem~2 of Sarkar~\cite{sarkar2011low}, \emph{``Low Distortion Delaunay Embedding of Trees in Hyperbolic Plane''}. Sarkar gives an explicit constructive embedding of any weighted tree into the 2-dimensional Poincar\'{e} disk $\mathbb{B}^2$ with multiplicative distortion at most $1+\varepsilon$, achieved by recursively placing children of each node on a hyperbolic circle and rescaling edges by a factor $\tau$ depending on $\varepsilon$. The construction uses
\[
\tau \;=\; \frac{1+\varepsilon/2}{1-\varepsilon/2},
\]
and a tighter analysis~\cite{sala2018representation} extends it to $\mathbb{B}^d$ with $d = O(\varepsilon^{-2})$, independent of $n$. Applying this construction to the safety taxonomy $\mathcal{T}$ proves part (b). \qed

\subsubsection{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:ot-margin}}

\paragraph{Part (i): from Sinkhorn divergence to margin.}
The Sinkhorn divergence $S_\varepsilon$ admits the well-known dual representation~\cite{genevay2018learning,feydy2019interpolating}:
\[
S_\varepsilon(P,Q) \;=\; \sup_{f, g \in \mathcal{F}_\varepsilon} \Big\{ \mathbb{E}_{x \sim P}[f(x)] \;+\; \mathbb{E}_{y \sim Q}[g(y)] \Big\},
\]
where the feasible set $\mathcal{F}_\varepsilon$ enforces an entropic constraint that, for cost $c(x,y) = \tfrac12\|x-y\|^2$ on a bounded domain, restricts $f,g$ to be approximately 1-Lipschitz: there exists $C_1>0$ such that any optimal pair $(f^*, g^*)$ satisfies $\|\nabla f^*\|_\infty \le 1 + C_1\sqrt{\varepsilon}$. Furthermore, by the entropic Kantorovich duality, $f^* = -g^*$ on $\mathrm{supp}(P) \cap \mathrm{supp}(Q)$ up to a constant.

Let $f^* := f^*_{a,b}$ be the optimal Sinkhorn potential for the pair $(Z_a, Z_b)$. Define the centered separator
\[
\bar f(x) \;:=\; f^*(x) - \tfrac12\!\left(\mathbb{E}_{Z_a}[f^*] + \mathbb{E}_{Z_b}[f^*]\right).
\]
Then $\bar f$ is $(1+C_1\sqrt{\varepsilon})$-Lipschitz, and
\begin{align*}
\mathbb{E}_{Z_a}[\bar f] - \mathbb{E}_{Z_b}[\bar f]
&= \mathbb{E}_{Z_a}[f^*] - \mathbb{E}_{Z_b}[f^*] \\
&= S_\varepsilon(Z_a, Z_b) - O(\varepsilon) \;\ge\; \Delta - O(\varepsilon).
\end{align*}

Applying Markov's inequality and the diameter bound $\mathrm{diam}(Z_c) \le 2r_c$, we obtain a population margin
\[
\gamma_{a,b} \;\ge\; \tfrac12 \Delta - (r_a + r_b) - C_1 \sqrt{\varepsilon},
\]
after rescaling $\bar f$ to be 1-Lipschitz. The factor $\tfrac12$ arises because the margin is measured from the midpoint of the two class means, not their gap. \qed

\paragraph{Part (ii): from margin to generalization.}
Given a 1-Lipschitz separator $f$ with population margin $\gamma_{a,b}$ as in (i), apply the standard Lipschitz-margin Rademacher bound~\cite{bartlett2002rademacher,koltchinskii2002empirical}: for a sample of size $n$, with probability $\ge 1-\delta$,
\[
\mathrm{err}(f) \;\le\; \widehat{\mathrm{err}}_{\gamma}(f) + \frac{2\,\mathfrak{R}_n(\mathcal{F}_{1\text{-Lip}})}{\gamma_{a,b}} + \sqrt{\frac{\log(1/\delta)}{2n}}.
\]
For 1-Lipschitz functions on $\mathbb{S}^{255}$, the Rademacher complexity satisfies $\mathfrak{R}_n(\mathcal{F}_{1\text{-Lip}}) = O(1/\sqrt{n})$~\cite{bartlett2002rademacher}. Since $\widehat{\mathrm{err}}_\gamma(f) = 0$ when the empirical margin matches the population margin (which holds with high probability by part (i)), the claim follows with $C_2$ absorbing the universal constants. \qed

\subsubsection{Training--Evaluation Overlap Audit}
\label{app:overlap}

To verify the claim of strict data disjointness (\S\ref{sec:exp}, \S\ref{sec:discussion}), we conducted an 8-gram overlap audit between the full 389K training corpus and each of the 9 evaluation benchmarks. For every training sample and every test sample across all benchmarks, we computed the Jaccard similarity of their 8-gram sets using MinHash with 128 permutations. Any (train, test) pair exceeding 0.3 Jaccard similarity was flagged as a potential near-duplicate. Across all 9 benchmarks and the entire training corpus, zero pairs exceeded this threshold. Exact 8-gram overlap counts (stricter metric) similarly confirmed zero meaningful overlap. This audit provides hard evidence that DeepSafe's reported gains reflect generalization to unseen distributions, not in-distribution memorization.
